Where the relay controller switches: two curves on the (β1, β2) belief square

At ρ=0 (independent hop dynamics) with hop-decomposed observation, the belief provably factors into two scalars — β1 = P(hop1 = Bad), β2 = P(hop2 = Bad) — collapsing the routing/warm-standby POMDP to a 2D belief-MDP. Each panel shows the resulting decision geometry on the unit square.

1. Always-warm sub-model — theorem-backed

Standby forced warm every step ⇒ observation is action-independent ⇒ a clamp identity closes the induction: Δ(β) = clamp(d(β), −c_switch, +c_switch). d(β) is provably monotone in (β1, β2), so both curves are exact level sets of the same scalar field, nested, giving an exact hysteresis band.

Structure is concentrated at low β2 — hop2 is short-lived but severe, so once β2 rises even a little, path B is already bad enough that β1 stops mattering (the whole row becomes "bail," matching Stage 0 Finding 3's hop2-sensitive / hop1-tolerant asymmetry).

2. Full unconstrained model — numerically probed

All 4 actions available (route × keep-standby-warm). No proof backs this field's monotonicity — action-dependent observability makes the value-of-information term hump-shaped, breaking the increasing-differences condition Topkis' theorem needs — but it verified monotone in every scenario tried. The warm-choice, unlike routing, is not a threshold: it forms a probing band right at the routing boundary.

Thin violet trace: one Monte-Carlo trajectory's true joint belief, projected to (β1, β2) — it loops through the band on its way to bailing and back, the hysteresis loop made visible. Bail curve here sits measurably further out (higher β1) than panel 1's — the "price of probing freedom": having the *option* to let the standby go cold later makes staying on B more attractive now.
